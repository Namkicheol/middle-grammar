Be a Wise Consumer!

Do you think you always make smart choices when you shop?
Well, you may believe so, but think again!
Companies use clever sales techniques which affect your buying choices.
Understanding these methods will help you become a wiser consumer.

Scarcity Marketing

Minji: What? The discount ends in three hours?
If I don't buy the shoes now, I will have to pay the full price later.
Wait, Minji! You're buying the shoes only because you don't want to miss the deal.
That's exactly how scarcity marketing works.
When people can only buy something for a short time, they feel a strong urge to get it.
Don't worry about missing the sale, Minji.
A similar sale will come back very soon.

Bandwagon Effect

Jiwon: That jacket is all over social media these days.
Everyone seems to be wearing it.
I really need to get that jacket, too!
Hold on, Jiwon!
You want it simply because you keep seeing it on your feed.
Many people fall for this kind of trend marketing every day.
A product's popularity on social media can grow as fast as a spreading rumor.
Once something goes viral, people start wanting it without thinking clearly.
Jiwon, a trendy item is not always the best choice for you.

Decoy Pricing

Sujin: I'm looking for a backpack for school.
Salesperson: How about this premium model?
Sujin: That's too expensive! I was thinking around 40,000 won.
Salesperson: This mid-range model is very popular, too.
Sujin: Oh, that feels much more reasonable!
Wait, Sujin! Your budget was 40,000 won, but you're about to buy a 55,000-won backpack.
The 55,000-won bag only seems cheap because the salesperson showed you an 80,000-won one first.
This is a classic example of decoy pricing.
Usually, an extreme option is placed first to make the middle option look like a great deal.
In your case, the expensive model was the decoy.
Sujin, always compare prices based on your own budget, not the first number you see.
